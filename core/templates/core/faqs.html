📋 Booking & Requirements for Tourists in Kenya
Do I need an international driving permit to rent a car in Kenya?▾
Yes. Tourists and foreign visitors driving in Kenya must hold a valid International Driving Permit (IDP) issued in their home country, alongside their original national driving licence. Both documents must be presented at vehicle pickup. Kenyan residents only need their national driving licence.
Can tourists rent a car in Kenya?▾
Absolutely. Munwan Car Rental welcomes international tourists. You'll need a valid passport, an International Driving Permit with your national licence, and a credit/debit card or M-Pesa for payment. We can meet you at Jomo Kenyatta International Airport (JKIA) with a name board the moment you land.
What documents are required to rent a car in Kenya?▾
You'll need three things:

1. A valid driving licence — national licence for residents, or national licence plus an International Driving Permit for tourists.
2. A passport or national ID.
3. A payment method — card, M-Pesa, Apple Pay, or bank transfer.

No notarisation or translation is required for licences issued in English.
What is the minimum age to rent a car in Kenya?▾
The minimum age to rent a car with Munwan Car Rental is 23 years old, with at least 2 years of driving experience. Drivers under 25 may pay a small young-driver surcharge. The maximum age is 75 subject to a valid licence and health.
Can I rent a car in Kenya with a foreign license?▾
Yes, provided your foreign licence is accompanied by an International Driving Permit (IDP). Licences issued in English may be accepted for short rentals without an IDP — please confirm with our team via WhatsApp before arrival to avoid any pickup delays.

🧭 Safety & Driving in Kenya (for Tourists)
Is it safe to drive in Kenya as a tourist?▾
Yes, Kenya is generally safe for tourist self-drive, especially main routes between Nairobi, Maasai Mara, Amboseli, and the coastal region. Key tips: observe speed limits, avoid night driving on highways, fuel up before long trips, keep doors locked in traffic, and keep valuables out of sight. All Munwan Car Rental vehicles have GPS tracking and 24/7 emergency support for peace of mind.
Which side of the road do people drive on in Kenya?▾
Kenya drives on the left side of the road (like the UK, Australia, India, and South Africa). Right-hand-drive vehicles are standard. Roundabouts give way to traffic from the right. If you're from a right-side-driving country (US, Europe), allow a little time to adjust — our with-driver option is ideal for your first day to avoid stress.
Are roads in Kenya good for self-drive?▾
Main highways in Kenya (Nairobi to Mombasa, Nairobi to Naivasha, Nairobi to Nyeri) are paved and well-signposted. Roads into national parks like Maasai Mara or Samburu are unpaved and sometimes rough — a 4x4 is essential. Urban Nairobi traffic can be heavy during rush hours; our GPS helps you navigate alternate routes.
Do I need a driver or can I self-drive in Kenya?▾
Both options are available:

• Self-drive is popular with confident drivers used to left-hand traffic and manual/automatic transmissions.
• With-driver is recommended if you're unfamiliar with Kenya's roads, plan complex safari itineraries, or want to fully enjoy the scenery.

Our vetted professional drivers start from $20/day extra.

Yes. After you enter your Safaricom number and click "Pay with M-Pesa", an STK Push prompt appears on your phone within a few seconds. Enter your 4-digit M-Pesa PIN to complete payment. If the prompt doesn't arrive, try the alternate manual Paybill option: Paybill 522533, Account: DK-BOOKING.

🌍 Tourist Tips: What to Know Before You Drive
Which side of the road do you drive on in Kenya?▾
Kenya drives on the left-hand side of the road, with the steering wheel on the right side of the vehicle. This is a legacy of British colonial influence and is the same as the UK, Australia, India, South Africa, and Japan. Visitors from the US, EU, and most of South America should expect a 1–2 day adjustment period. Roundabouts move clockwise — yield to traffic from the right.
Is it safe for tourists to self-drive in Kenya?▾
Yes, with sensible precautions. Kenya has a well-developed network of fuel stations, mobile coverage on all major routes, and 24/7 emergency services. Stick to daylight driving (we strongly advise against night driving outside Nairobi), keep your fuel topped up, lock doors in city traffic, and avoid driving in flood-prone areas during heavy rain. Our 24/7 roadside assistance covers the entire country.
Can I drive in Kenya with my UK / US / EU / Australian licence?▾
Yes. Visitors from the UK, USA, EU countries, Australia, New Zealand, Canada, South Africa, India, and most Commonwealth nations can drive in Kenya for up to 90 days using their home country licence (must be in English) accompanied by an International Driving Permit. Licences in non-Latin scripts must always be translated via an IDP. After 90 days, you'll need to convert to a Kenyan licence through NTSA.
What happens if a police officer stops me at a checkpoint?▾
Police checkpoints are routine and usually quick. Pull over calmly, lower your window, and politely greet the officer (a "Habari" — hello — goes a long way). Have ready: your passport, IDP, home licence, and the vehicle's rental agreement & insurance certificate (we provide these in your dashboard glove compartment). Most checks are documentary and last under a minute. Do not pay any "fines" on the spot — request a written ticket and call us if anything seems unusual.
Should I drive at night in Kenya?▾
No, we strongly recommend against night driving outside Nairobi and Mombasa city limits. Kenyan roads have unmarked livestock, pedestrians, potholes, and unlit broken-down trucks. National park access is also restricted after dusk. Plan to be at your destination by 6pm. If you must drive at night, stay on well-lit highways like Mombasa Road or Thika Road, drive at moderate speed, and use full beams cautiously.
What are the speed limits in Kenya?▾
Kenyan speed limits in 2025:

• Urban areas / built-up zones: 50 km/h
• Open highways (single carriageway): 80 km/h
• Dual carriageways & expressways (Nairobi Expressway, Thika Road): 110 km/h
• National parks: 50 km/h, often lower near wildlife
• Schools & townships: 30–50 km/h

Speed cameras are common on Mombasa Road, Thika Road, and the Nairobi Expressway. Fines start at KES 10,000.
How much are Kenya national park entry fees in 2025?▾
Park entry fees are paid per person, per 24-hour period at the gate, separate from your car rental. Approximate non-resident rates:

• Maasai Mara National Reserve: USD 100/adult, USD 50/child
• Amboseli National Park: USD 60/adult
• Lake Nakuru National Park: USD 60/adult
• Tsavo East / West: USD 52/adult
• Nairobi National Park: USD 43/adult
• Hell's Gate / Lake Naivasha area: USD 26/adult

Pay by Visa/Mastercard at the gate, or M-Pesa where supported. Carry your passport for non-resident verification.

For peak safari season (July–October for the Great Migration, and December–early January for holidays), book at least 2–3 months ahead. Toyota Prados, Land Cruisers, and pop-top safari vehicles get reserved fastest. Off-season (April–June, November), 2–3 weeks ahead is usually enough. Will I have mobile signal and GPS in the parks?▾
Safaricom 4G covers all major roads and most park gates. Inside Maasai Mara, Amboseli, and Tsavo, signal is patchy but available at major lodges and main tracks. Samburu and Lewa have weaker coverage. Every Munwan Car Rental vehicle includes a GPS unit pre-loaded with offline Kenya maps, so you can navigate even in low-signal zones. We also recommend downloading Maps.me or Google Maps offline before your trip.
Where do I find fuel on safari routes?▾
Fuel is widely available on tarmac roads. Major brands: Total, Shell, Rubis, Vivo. Fuel up at the last town before entering a park — for the Mara, that's Narok (90 km from the gate). For Amboseli, Emali. There is no fuel inside the parks. Carry a 20 L jerry can on multi-day safaris in remote areas. Petrol is "premium" or "regular"; diesel is widely available. Prices fluctuate monthly (KES 180–210/L typical in 2025).

Can I drive in Kenya during the rainy season?▾
Yes, but with a 4×4. The "long rains" (March–May) and "short rains" (October–November) make tarmac slippery and turn dirt tracks to mud — especially around Maasai Mara, Amboseli, and the Aberdares. We recommend a Toyota Prado or Land Cruiser in these months. Avoid driving immediately after heavy rain (flash flooding on bridges in Tana and Athi River basins). Most highways stay passable; pause for the worst storms (1–2 hours).
Do I need a Kenya visa or eTA?▾
As of January 2024, Kenya replaced visas with the Electronic Travel Authorisation (eTA). All visitors except citizens of East African Community countries (Kenya, Uganda, Tanzania, Rwanda, Burundi, South Sudan) need an eTA. Apply at etakenya.go.ke at least 3 days before travel. The fee is USD 32.50, valid for 90 days. You'll need: passport (6+ months validity), return ticket, proof of accommodation, and a digital photo. Approval is usually within 24–72 hours.

What should I do in case of an accident?▾
Stay calm and follow these steps:

1. Check for injuries. Call 999 (police/ambulance) if anyone is hurt.
2. Move the vehicle off the road if safe. Place warning triangles (50 m front and rear).
3. Do not admit fault or sign anything from the other party.
4. Take photos of the scene, both vehicles, both number plates, and any injuries.
5. Get the other driver's details: name, ID, phone, vehicle registration, insurance.
6. File a police report (P3 form) at the nearest station — required for insurance.
7. Call Munwan Car Rental immediately on +254 727 745 907. We handle the rest.

Our entire Munwan Car Rental team speaks fluent English. Learning a few Swahili phrases like "Habari" (hello), "Asante" (thank you), and "Pole pole" (slowly) is appreciated but not necessary.
